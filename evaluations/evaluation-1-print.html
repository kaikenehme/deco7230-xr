01Objective and validation metrics

Category: Creation

Interactive Prototype 1 is a VR living room (Unity 6, XR Interaction Toolkit, controllers) in which the floor and sofa are staying and everything else can change. The concept's hero interaction is a hold-up loop: touch a kept surface to pull a colour sample off it, hold the sample against a wall to preview a harmonised paint, twist the wrist to tune it, release to commit. Alongside it, for comparison only, sits a conventional controller menu with a 24-colour Dulux catalogue, floor materials and eight pieces of furniture. Secondary diegetic controls: a lamp that cycles warm / cool / daylight when touched, and a scheme save-and-cycle.

The testing plan set three assumptions. A2 was the primary objective — the whole concept rests on it, and it could genuinely fail.

The plan fixed the measures and the 20-second prompt rule but did not set numeric pass marks; I judged each aim by the direction and consistency of the evidence across participants, and I say so in §4 rather than fit thresholds after the fact. Protocol per participant (~5 min): brief → 30 s uninstructed free look → Task 1 ("repaint this room so it works with the floor and sofa you're keeping") → Task 2 (change the floor, add and move furniture, save and switch schemes) → four post-test questions. One paper sheet per participant, 16 rows; the sheet is in Appendix D.

Setting — read before the results. The studio session ran on the XR Device Simulator inside the Unity Editor on a laptop, with mouse and keyboard standing in for headset and controllers (grip = G, trigger = click, wrist twist = mouse-driven controller rotation). The borrowed Quest stayed unauthorized over USB and the built APK was never sideloaded. Every "controller" action below is therefore a mouse action. This confounds every discoverability measure, and I treat it as such throughout.

03Analysis and insights

Five participants across two settings is a small sample, so I report counts, not percentages, and I only call something a pattern when it held for every participant it applied to.

The loop's entry gesture happens; the loop never starts (5/5, 0/5). Everyone touched the floor or sofa during free look — the exact gesture that begins the hold-up loop — and nobody pulled a sample. Touching a kept surface produces no response that reads as "you can take this". This held in the studio, at home, with a facilitator who had already run the protocol twice, and with participants who probably knew what the project was. It is the strongest thing IP1 produced, and it is a failure of affordance, not of the concept: the interaction was never reached, so it was never judged.

The menu wins by default, not by preference (menu 5/5; unprompted 0/3 recorded). Both routes to a paint choice were invisible. The menu produced every kept choice only because the facilitator's fifth scripted prompt ("point at the floor and press the trigger") reveals it directly, and nothing in the room reveals the hold-up loop. This is a discoverability result, and I do not read it as "people prefer menus".

Prompt load was constant (5, 3 · 4, 2, 4). Roughly one intervention per minute against a 20-second stuck rule, unchanged by facilitator practice or a familiar setting — the prototype, not the session, is what needs prompting.

Confusion is about manipulation, not choice (5/5). Every "confusing thing", every row-11 answer and every observer note is about moving things and reading feedback: the controller (×3), drag-and-drop (×2), the selection highlight (×2), rotating furniture, and — said independently by P2 and P4 in almost the same words — that selecting furniture should place it, not drop it at your feet. Nobody was confused by colours, options or the room. Some of this is simulator cost (a mouse standing in for a hand), but place-versus-drop and the highlight are prototype decisions and would be the same on a headset.

The diegetic lamp can be found — but wasn't in the studio (0/2 vs 2/3). Two flatmates touched the lamp unprompted; neither studio participant did. The data cannot separate a practised facilitator, prior exposure to the project, and more relaxed free look. Scheme save-and-cycle was found by nobody (0/4) in either setting.

"Limited options" split once n grew — about the wrong thing. Studio: "it's good", "enough for now". Home: "feels good for now" against "feels a bit restrictive" and "need more painting options". All five were rating the unconstrained 24-colour catalogue. That says nothing about A2 directly, but it is a warning: if 24 free colours already feel thin to two of five people, a seven-option harmony set will read as fewer unless the constraint visibly explains itself.

06Reflection — concept, method, and the next round

What worked. The uninstructed phase did its job: it produced an unambiguous negative on the entry gesture that a guided demo would have hidden. Separating "did" from "said" on the sheet made the pattern in §3 easy to see. Adding three real supplementary sessions was cheap and turned a 2/2 observation into a 5/5 one without changing any verdict — which is itself worth knowing.

What didn't. The platform. A body interaction was tested through a mouse, five times, because the Quest would not authorise over USB on the day. This is also the pipeline risk for IP2b, so it gets solved first, not last. Solo facilitation with a paper tally lost about a third of the studio rows; at home, with practice, it lost almost none — but the same instrument gaps recurred because I did not revise the sheet between rounds.

Instrument. Three fixes for IP2a's sheet: row 10 branches (ask about constraint only if the constrained set was used; otherwise ask why the menu); row 11 is reworded so it stops collecting "what was hard"; the "trusted method" question is printed, not carried in the plan. Prompt numbers, not ticks, go in the tally boxes so I know which prompt unstuck whom. Screen-record, with consent, so lost rows can be recovered.

Sample. Flatmates got n to 5 but bring prior exposure, which may explain the lamp result. Next round: recruit outside the flat and log whether the participant has seen the project before. Budget seven minutes per participant, not five — setup and briefing ate the studio's time.

Next. IP2a on the headset, with the entry affordance rebuilt, Task 1 starting sample-in-hand, and A2 — still the primary aim — finally measured.
