01Objective and validation metrics

Category: Creation

The prototype. A furnished virtual living room. The floor and sofa are staying; the walls can change. The hero interaction: touch a kept surface to pull off a colour sample, hold it up against a wall to preview a matching paint, twist the wrist to step through 7 matching options, let go to commit. A normal menu with the full paint catalogue is available too, as the comparison. New since IP1: a visible tab and glow when you touch a kept surface, furniture you can grab, place and rotate, a clock that changes the daylight, a lamp, and three preset rooms.

Session (~7 min each): brief → 45 s silent free look → Task 1, starting with a floor sample already in hand: "Repaint this room so it works with the floor and sofa" → Task 2: move the armchair, add a piece, check another time of day → 6 interview questions, logged live on an iPad. No numeric pass marks were set, so aims are judged on how consistent the evidence is.

Platform change. The testing plan says desktop mode (mouse and keyboard) because the headset had failed to connect before IP1. On the day it connected, so all five sessions ran on the Quest 3S. The plan is left as written. This means IP2a is real headset data, but any comparison with IP1 (simulator) mixes a design change with a platform change.

03Analysis and insights

1 · The way in now works. Everyone noticed the tab or glow and 4 of 5 pulled a sample; in IP1 the same touch led nowhere for all 5. Caution: the headset also changed, and a hint may have come first for some, so I can't say how much is the new design alone.
2 · People preview with the sample but decide with the menu. All five held a matching sample; all five kept a menu colour, saying it was easier. Why: nobody saw more than 2 of the 7 options, and 2 of 5 never found the twist that shows the rest. It was two hidden colours against a whole visible catalogue. That tests visibility, not whether limiting options helps.
3 · People judged on lightness, the generator varies hue. All three rejections were about lightness ("light", "black"), and nobody mentioned the floor or sofa. The colour generator changes hue in 5 of 7 options and leaves out off-whites, which is what most real walls are.
4 · The menu wins on effort, the room wins on feel. Menu-trusters said "easier" and "intuitive". The one room-truster wanted more physical interaction (a physical lamp, throwing colour at the wall). The hold-up route loses for as long as it is also the slower one.
5 · Furniture is solved, it needs tuning. IP1's biggest complaint is gone (5/5 placed, 5/5 rotated). What's left is "too sensitive" and "the drag".
6 · Time of day was used because they were told to. Everyone used the clock, but the task and a hint asked them to. Neither of the 2 asked changed a decision.

06Reflection and next steps

What workedWhat didn'tWhat I'll change
  • Switching to the headset gave real reach and scale data
  • The device pipeline now works (72 fps), removing IP2b's biggest risk
  • The iPad logger lost no rows and logged which hint was used
  • Starting Task 1 with a sample in hand got everyone to the matching colours
  • The platform switch broke comparison with the plan and with IP1
  • My interview branch skipped the primary question
  • Phase timers unused, so hint timing was lost
  • Studio peers only; 2 had seen the project
  • Branch questions on exposure, not outcome
  • One "next phase" button that timestamps everything
  • IP2b plan: standardised questionnaire, ≥5 academic references, the five design principles
  • Build: fan deck, lightness palette, toss-to-commit, real light

The main lesson. Twice now, the primary aim went unanswered for a reason unrelated to the concept: in IP1 people never reached the constrained colours; in IP2a they reached them but were never asked about them. Both times I designed the test around the path I expected people to take. For IP2b, every decisive question is asked of everyone who was exposed to the thing it asks about, whatever they chose. The platform switch was the right call for data quality, but I'll decide the platform before writing the plan, not on the day.

CData-quality and transcription notes

  • Prompt timing and order are unreliable (Appendix B). Which prompts were used is reliable; when, and in which phase, is not.
  • Row 3 against prompt 3. P1, P2, P3 and P5 are logged "unprompted" in row 3, and each also has prompt 3 ("press the trigger on the little colour tab") in their list. Since Task 1 begins with a sample placed in the hand, prompt 3 may have been used later to pull a second sample, but the log cannot show this. A1′ is reported as provisional for this reason.
  • Prompt 8 for all five. Clock use (row 12) followed both the Task 2 instruction and, at some point, prompt 8, so it is not a discovery measure.
  • Prompt 6 for P2, P4, P5 reveals the menu, so for those three the menu was shown at some point although row 14 (Task 2) logs it opened unprompted. P1 and P3 reached the menu without prompt 6. Prompt 7 for P5 names the thumbstick, although row 11 logs rotate as unprompted.
  • Row 5 was typed in whole minutes, not read off the stopwatch. P4 and P5's 4 min exceed the 2-minute task: either the task ran long or "4" means "not within the task".
  • Row 17a was never asked. It branched on row 8 = hold-up or both; row 8 was menu for all five. This is an instrument design error, not a missing entry.
  • Blanks are blanks, not "no": P4 row 3, P3 row 9, P2 row 12 (yes/no), and several of rows 17–21.
  • The logger's prompt wording had been reworded for desktop mode; on the headset the facilitator spoke the controller versions above.
  • P1 and P2's records were created on Thu 24 Sep (dry-run entries) and overwritten on the day; all values shown were entered on 25 Sep.
  • Observer notes in §2 are lightly tidied for spelling; the raw text is: P1 "different iteraction with the lamp (fisical interaction) · Gaming interaction like ballon trough in the wall and change the colour"; P2 "Other types of room"; P4 "Too sensitive the fortitude moving · Swap for the menu" ("fortitude" read as an autocorrect of "furniture").
