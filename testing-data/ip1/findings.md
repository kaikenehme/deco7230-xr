## 2. Patterns (Synthesis)

Five participants across two settings is still a small sample; these are consistent observations, not themes with statistical weight. Each is given as studio count + supplementary count.

**P-a. The hold-up loop was never entered — 0/5 (0+0).** Every participant touched the floor or sofa during free look (row 2 = yes, 5/5) — the *entry gesture* happened five times out of five — and nobody pulled a sample or held anything against a wall. Twist-to-tune, which sits behind that step, was found by 0/3 recorded. Touching a kept surface produces no response the participant reads as "you can take this". This held in both settings, with three more sessions, and with a facilitator who had already run the protocol twice. It is the strongest result IP1 produced.

**P-b. The menu was the route every time — 5/5 (2+3) — and never unprompted where recorded.** Rows 13 and 14 = menu for all five. Of the three sheets that record *how* the menu was reached, all three say "prompted" (P1, P2, P4); P3 and P5 don't mark it. Both routes to a paint choice were undiscoverable; the menu won because prompt #5 ("point at the floor and press the trigger") reveals it directly.

**P-c. Prompt load stayed high — 5, 3 · 4, 2, 4.** Mean 3.6 prompts in a ~5-minute session against a 20-second stuck threshold. The supplementary sessions were not easier despite a practised facilitator and participants who probably knew what the project was.

**P-d. Secondary controls: the lamp *was* found at home, nothing else was.** Studio: lamp 0/2, grab 0/2, save/cycle 0/1. Supplementary: **lamp 2/3 unprompted** (P4, P5), grab 1/3 unprompted (P4), save/cycle 0/3. The diegetic lamp — the concept's flagship "no menus" control — went unfound in the studio and was found by two of three flatmates. The data cannot say why: a practised facilitator, participants who had heard about the lamp, or more time in free look are all live (platform was the same, so that is ruled out). Scheme save/cycle: **0/4 recorded** across both settings — never found once.

**P-e. Confusion is about *manipulation*, not *choice* — 5/5.** "One confusing thing": simulator, drag-and-drop, controller ×3. Row 11 answers (highlight, drag-and-drop, furniture selection) and all five observer notes (highlight colour ×2, rotate furniture, place-vs-drop ×2, furniture options) are about moving things and reading feedback. Two participants (P2, P4) independently said the same sentence: selecting furniture should *place* it, not drop it. Nobody was confused by colours, options, or the room.

**P-f. Opinions on "limited options" split once n grew — but every answer is about the wrong thing.** Studio: "it's good", "enough for now". Supplementary: "feels good for now" vs **"feels a bit restrictive"** and **"need more painting options"**. All five chose via the menu (P-b), so all five are rating the **24-colour Dulux catalogue**, not the harmony-constrained set that the question was written for. See §3 A2 — this is not evidence for or against A2, but it is a warning sign (§5).

**P-g. Furniture wants more of everything.** P5 asked for more furniture options (catalogue has 8 pieces) and a room "already set up"; P3/P1 want the selection highlight changed; P2/P4 want place-not-drop. Furniture was meant to be static/shallow in IP1 (phasing table) but it is where participants spent their opinions.

## 3. Assumptions — verdicts (Evaluation of Aims)

| Assumption | Verdict | Grounds | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** — loop understood without being told | **Invalidated on the simulator** | 0/5 pulled a sample (0+0); 0/3 recorded found the menu unprompted; 2–5 prompts each | Moderate. 5/5 across two settings, two participant pools and a practised facilitator. The simulator confound (§4) still caps the claim at "invalidated with a mouse", not "invalidated in VR" |
| **A2** — constraint helps rather than frustrates (*primary*) | **Untested** | No participant ever held a harmonised sample, so the tuned options were never seen — in either setting. Row 10 answers (5/5) describe the unconstrained catalogue. Confidence scores (3, 3, 4, 2) are for menu choices. | n/a — no data on the actual assumption. Three extra sessions did not change this because the loop was never reached |
| **A3** — which method is used, which is trusted | **Half answered** | Used: menu 5/5, always after prompting where recorded, and only because the hold-up loop was invisible. Trusted: the question was never printed on the sheet, so not asked of any of the five. | Low. "Menu wins" is a discoverability result, not a preference result |

**Honest summary for the report:** five sessions tested the prototype's *discoverability on the simulator* and found the hold-up loop undiscoverable 5/5. They did **not** test the concept's central claim (A2), and adding three participants did not help because the gate to A2 — pulling a sample — was never opened. Say that plainly. A clean "A2 could not be tested and here is exactly why, twice" is worth more on the Interpretation and Reflection criteria than a soft "A2 looked okay".

## 4. Limitations (put these in Results/Reflection, not a footnote)

1. **n = 5, split 2 + 3 across settings.** Report counts, not percentages. The supplementary sessions are out-of-class: state date, place, platform and that the participants were flatmates.
2. **Simulator, not headset — all five sessions.** Mouse/keyboard emulation removes the body from an interaction designed around the body. "The controller" ×3 and "simulator is confusing" may be simulator cost, prototype cost, or both; the data cannot separate them.
3. **Convenience sample with prior exposure.** Flatmates live with the person building the thing. The lamp result (P-d) in particular may reflect having heard about the lamp, not having discovered it.
4. **Instrument fixes not applied.** `followup-sessions.md` listed four fixes (trusted-method row, branched row 10, prompt numbers in boxes, screen recording). None was made before P3–P5 ran. Rows are therefore comparable with P1/P2 but the same gaps recur: no "trusted method" data (A3), row 10 still mis-targeted, which prompts were used still unknown.
5. **Missing measures.** Studio: rows 3 (P1), 5, 7 (P2), 9 (P2), 11 (both). Supplementary: row 15 "opened unprompted?" for P3, P5. Row 11 was answered as "what was hard", not "why was a colour rejected", 3/3.
6. **No recording, any session.** No way to recover missing rows, check tallies, or resolve the two cursive readings flagged in `README.md` ("framed", "highlight").

## 5. What this means for the design (Insights → Concept Iteration)

| Insight | Design response | Which prototype |
|---|---|---|
| Touching a kept surface does nothing visible → loop never starts, 5/5 | Kept surfaces need a persistent *pullable* affordance: a swatch tab hovering at the touch point, a hand-glow on proximity, sample "peels" on trigger. First-contact feedback, not a tutorial. | IP2a (fix the weakest thing from IP1 — this is it, confirmed twice) |
| Both routes undiscoverable; the one the prompt names wins | Diegetic ≠ discoverable. Add a 10-second in-world onboarding: floor/sofa pulse once with a "staying" label on entry. | IP2a |
| Complaints are about manipulation (place/drop ×2, rotate, highlight ×2, controller ×3) | Furniture: grab = hold, release = *place* (P2 and P4's own words); rotate on thumbstick; selection highlight as a clear outline, not a frame tint. | IP2a |
| Two of five called a **24-colour catalogue** "restrictive" / "need more" | Risk flag for A2, not a verdict: if 24 unconstrained colours already feel thin, a 7-option harmony set may read as *fewer*, not *better* — unless the constraint is visibly *doing* something (showing why each option matches). The harmony set must explain itself in-world. Test this directly in IP2a. | IP2a testing plan |
| A2 still untested after 5 sessions — the gate never opens | Do not rely on free discovery to reach A2. Task 1 in IP2a starts with a sample already in hand, so every participant reaches the constrained set and row 10 finally measures what it was written for. | IP2a testing plan |
| Lamp: 0/2 studio, 2/3 home; save/cycle 0/4 | Keep the lamp (it *can* be found) but add a glow + pull-cord so studio-condition users find it too. Scheme save/cycle: never found — move to a visible object (a pinboard on the wall) or cut from the IP2a test. | IP2a |
| Furniture is where opinions went (8 pieces; "more options", "room already set up") | Furniture was shallow by design in IP1. IP2a already promises grab/move/rotate; add a pre-furnished starting room so the task is *re-arranging*, not *furnishing from empty*. | IP2a |

## 6. Reflection points (for §6 of the template)

- **Method:** solo facilitation + paper tally lost ~30% of rows in the studio; at home with a practised hand it lost almost none — but the *same* instrument gaps recurred because the sheet was not revised between runs. Lesson: fix the instrument before the next session, not after the report.
- **Platform:** the simulator turned a body-interaction test into a mouse test, five times. IP2a must be on the headset — this is also the pipeline risk for the IP2b hurdle, so solve the `unauthorized` Quest problem now (studio staff / developer mode / a different cable).
- **Instrument:** row 10 must branch — ask about constraint only if the loop was used; otherwise ask why the menu was chosen. Row 11 needs rewording — three participants answered it as "what was hard". The "trusted method" question must be printed.
- **Sample:** three flatmates got n to 5 cheaply; the cost is prior exposure and a home setting that may explain the lamp result. Next round: recruit outside the flat, and log whether the participant has seen the project before.
- **Positive:** the uninstructed phase did its job again — five sessions, two settings, one unambiguous negative on the entry gesture. That is the most useful thing IP1 produced, and it is what IP2a's "rebuild the weakest thing" is now aimed at.
